# Policy context: YES-TECH and the field-level validation gap

Why this project's per-field measurement matters outside the benchmark.

**Everything below is sourced from the official manual or named reporting.
Nothing here is inferred from the SICKLE results.** The method used to check it
is recorded at the end so it can be repeated.

---

## 1. India is already automating satellite yield estimation for insurance

**YES-TECH** (Yield Estimation System based on Technology), Ministry of
Agriculture & Farmers Welfare, rolled out from **Kharif 2023**. It blends
satellite/weather-model yield estimates with manual Crop Cutting Experiments
(CCEs) to set insurance payouts under PMFBY.

| | |
|---|---|
| Scale reported for 2025–26 | **344 districts, 12 states** |
| National minimum | **≥30% weightage** to modelled yield |
| Upper cap on modelled weightage | **none** (§5.2.1.3) |
| Madhya Pradesh | **100% technology-based** |
| Maharashtra, Uttar Pradesh | 50% |
| Odisha, Karnataka | 40% |

> §5.2.1.1 — *"At least 30% weightage is to be assigned to modelled yield.
> Composite yield estimate will be generated by assigning 70% weightage to CCE
> yield and 30% weightage to modelled yield"*

> §5.2.1.3 — *"States can increase the weightage for modelled yield at their
> discretion and **there is no upper cap**"*

This is not a pilot. Payouts are being decided this way now.

## 2. The spatial unit is the village, and nothing finer appears anywhere

> *"generate reliable & scalable technology-based yield estimates for paddy and
> wheat crops at the **smallest administrative unit, i.e., Gram Panchayat**"*

Searching the **full OCR of all 116 pages** for any sub-village unit:

| term | occurrences | what they actually are |
|---|---:|---|
| `plot level` | **0** | — |
| `individual farmer` | **0** | — |
| `farmer level` | **0** | — |
| `per farmer` | **0** | — |
| `sub-IU` | **0** | — |
| `cadastr` | 1 | a readiness questionnaire: *"Whether Cadastral map is available?"* |
| `parcel` | 1 | the idiom *"part and parcel of the present agreement"* |
| `field level` | 1 | a contractor staffing rule: *"capacity to work in field level"* |
| `Insurance Unit` | 15 | the unit of assessment |
| `Gram Panchayat` / `GP level` | 7 | the unit of assessment |
| `CCE` | 118 | the validation reference |

**In 116 pages there is not one requirement to validate below the Insurance
Unit.** The three apparent hits are a checklist question, a figure of speech,
and a staffing requirement.

## 3. Accuracy metrics are named; almost no numeric bar is set

Metrics appear as items to **report**, not as pass/fail criteria:

> *"Accuracy Evaluation (RMSE, NRMSE, t-test, MAPE, correlation coefficient,
> Index of Agreement, etc.)"* — report templates, pp. 46–47

> *"Statistical criteria need to be considered for accuracy, such as the
> mathematical expectation of mean absolute of forecast error MAE, RMSE or
> NRMSE"* — p. 58

**The only numeric accuracy bars found in all 116 pages** apply to crop
*mapping*, not yield, and are evaluated at IU average:

> p. 54 — *"It is to be ensured that **commission errors are <10%** and
> **omission errors can be accommodated up to 20%**. By adopting this strategy
> on mapping error, the IU average crop health…"*

And the requirement for yield accuracy is circular:

> p. 16 — *"minimum 30% weightage of Technology driven yield … **with desired
> level of statistical accuracy, as stipulated in YES-TECH manual**"*

The manual states that accuracy is stipulated in the manual, without
stipulating a figure for yield.

**Note:** a separate, shorter *"Manual for Implementation"* (48 pp., January
2023) does set one — *"The error (nRMSE) … should not be more than 30% in
historical data"*, at IU level, §4.3.6. **That sentence does not appear in the
116-page official manual.** Which document governs should be checked before
citing it.

Every occurrence of the word *threshold* in the manual refers to **Threshold
Yield (TY)** — the insurance trigger — not to an accuracy threshold.

### Performance evaluation is of the contractor, and is deferred

§11 "Performance Evaluation of Technical Implementation Partner" assesses
*"the TIP's skills, commitment and efficiencies"*, then defers the indicators:

> §11.2 — *"a detailed Monitoring and Evaluation (M&E) framework … containing
> key performance indicators with assigned weightage, **will be devised and
> shared separately** by YES-TECH Committee."*

## 4. The estimates cannot be challenged or obtained

> §5.1.5 — *"**No challenges or appeals against the past years' yield estimates
> shall be entertained in any court of law or appellate authority.** Past
> estimates will fall within the category of 'intellectual property' under
> section 8(1)(d) of the Right to Information Act, 2005, and **not be made
> public**."*

So the estimates are not legally challengeable, are RTI-exempt, and are not
published. **Independent external validation is therefore not merely useful —
it is close to the only available check.**

## 5. What this project contributes

The gap is: **assessment and validation happen at village level; nothing
measures reliability within it.**

This project measures exactly that, on a published benchmark over the same
crop and region:

| | |
|---|---|
| Headline (pixel-pooled) IoU | **0.8665** |
| Median **individual field** | **0.672** |
| Fields below IoU 0.5 | **33%** |
| Seed-to-seed correlation of per-field IoU | **+0.53 to +0.58** |
| Fields failing in **every** independent run | **11 of 12** |

The last two matter most for policy: **failure is systematic, not random.** The
same fields are mapped badly every time. That makes **selective automation**
feasible — automate the reliable fields, route the rest to human verification —
which is not possible if failure is random.

### What must NOT be claimed

- That this project shows YES-TECH is inaccurate. **It does not.** It measures
  a published research benchmark (SICKLE), not YES-TECH's operational models.
- That a model error denies one farmer a payout. PMFBY assesses **per Insurance
  Unit** by design; all farmers in a village share the assessed yield. The
  correct argument is that **IU estimates are aggregated over fields, so
  systematic per-field error biases the village estimate**, and IU-level
  validation against CCE cannot detect it.
- Anything about interstate water disputes. SICKLE covers the Tamil Nadu
  Cauvery delta only (Coastal Cauvery, Coastal Vennar, Lower Cauvery, Middle
  Vennar, Upper Cauvery), 2018–2020. There is no Karnataka data.

## 6. How this was verified, so it can be repeated

1. Downloaded `https://www.ncfc.gov.in/downloads/YESTECH_Manual.pdf`
   (142 MB, 116 pages, "YES-TECH MANUAL 2023").
2. The file is a **scan with no text layer** — a keyword search returns zero
   hits and would wrongly suggest the terms are absent.
3. OCR'd all 116 pages (rapidocr-onnxruntime at 200 dpi) → **231,931
   characters**; searched that text.
4. Pages 26, 38 and the table of contents were additionally **read visually**
   and matched the OCR.

**Caveat:** OCR is imperfect. Before quoting any sentence in the thesis, open
the cited page and read it. The page numbers above are PDF page numbers.
